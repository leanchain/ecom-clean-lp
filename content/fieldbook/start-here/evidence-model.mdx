Reliable commerce work depends less on producing more findings and more on labeling what each finding actually means.

## Observed evidence

Observed evidence was captured directly from a controlled or named surface: a rendered product page, network response, catalog record, merchant diagnostic, transaction ledger, browser test, or timestamped AI answer.

Record the source, observation time, affected object, market, device, and test conditions when they matter.

## Attributed results

Attributed results belong to a platform's attribution model. Advertising and analytics systems may use different identity rules, windows, deduplication behavior, and modeled conversions.

<Callout type="boundary" title="Revenue boundary">
Attributed revenue is not automatically booked revenue. The commerce ledger remains the source for completed orders unless the organization has explicitly defined another accounting system.
</Callout>

## Modeled estimates

A forecast, opportunity estimate, expected impact, or probability is modeled. Display its assumptions and confidence. Never restate it as an observed result.

## Hypotheses

A hypothesis explains evidence and proposes something testable. It should name the controlled object that may change and the observation that would support or weaken the explanation.

## Verified outcomes

A verified outcome requires a defined intervention and a retest. Verification may show that a page now renders correctly, a feed agrees with the page, an event is no longer duplicated, or a monitored answer changed.

It does not automatically establish revenue causation. Commercial impact often requires a longer measurement design.

## Minimum evidence record

- Object or journey being investigated
- Source and collection method
- Timestamp and relevant market context
- Observed value
- Expected value or acceptance criterion
- Confidence and remaining uncertainty
- Owner and next verification step
