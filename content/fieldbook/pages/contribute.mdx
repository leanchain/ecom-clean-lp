The Fieldbook starts with reviewed contributions rather than a mostly empty forum. Accepted submissions become documents, resource entries, or anonymized case files.

## What to send

- A commerce problem we have missed
- A correction backed by an official source
- An agent skill or human checklist
- A playbook used across teams
- An open-source project, standard, or official reference
- An anonymized field result

## What makes a useful submission

Name the proposed page, the problem it addresses, who would use it, and the sources behind it. Include known limits and explain what the contribution adds to the current material.

## How we review it

We check whether the submission is useful, sourced, maintainable, properly attributed, legally usable, and safe to publish. Claims about rankings, revenue, or causation need especially strong support.

## Case files

A case file turns a verified commerce investigation into a reusable account of what happened, what changed, and how the result was checked. We publish one only when the source material can be shared safely and the evidence supports the claims being made.

A useful case file includes:

- The initial signal
- The affected product, page, event, or journey
- The evidence collected
- Explanations considered
- The change made
- The retest method
- The result
- Open questions

Remove customer, employee, transaction, and commercially sensitive data unless publication has been approved. Estimates stay labeled as estimates. A technical fix is not presented as a revenue win without a suitable measurement design.

<Callout type="evidence" title="No invented case studies">
Until a real case can be verified and published, the Fieldbook provides the method here rather than publishing fictional success stories.
</Callout>

If you have a publishable field result, include the dates, source material, exact change, and retest when you submit it.
